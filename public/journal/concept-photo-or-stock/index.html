Concept, stock or actual product: the caption matters

A clear image credit helps you understand what a picture can—and cannot—tell you.

BeeGoods Editorial · October 17, 2026

Three kinds of visual

A photograph of an actual product, licensed illustrative footage and a generated concept can all have a role on a developing website. They do different jobs. An actual product photograph can show the item being discussed. Stock can introduce a subject. A concept can make a design direction visible before a product or place is finished.

Do not let the picture overpromise

A concept honey jar is not proof of a harvest, source or finished package. A candle-making clip does not establish the ingredients of a BeeGoods product. A greenhouse illustration does not show a completed operating venue. Captions help keep those boundaries clear while allowing the site to remain visual and inviting.

What to look for here

BeeGoods labels its collection concepts and credits its stock footage. Confirmed product information will need to stand on its own when products are offered. If an image leaves you unsure about what is actually available, ask. The purpose of a brand story is to help someone understand an idea, not make them guess which parts have already been built.
