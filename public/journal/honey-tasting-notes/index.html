Make your own honey tasting notes

A simple way to remember what you enjoy without turning breakfast into a competition.

BeeGoods Editorial · October 17, 2026

Keep the comparison simple

Choose a small number of honeys you already have and label them clearly. Use the same kind of spoon and the same plain accompaniment for each. The point is to notice your own preferences, not to award an official score. If you change the bread, tea or serving temperature every time, you are comparing more than the honey.

Write what you actually notice

Use everyday words. You might describe the first impression, the texture and what remains after a taste. “Good with this bread” can be a more useful note than an impressive word you would never use again. Keep the jar name and the maker beside each note so you can find that particular product later. Your notes are personal observations, not laboratory results or proof of origin.

Build a table you want to return to

A tasting can be an easy gathering around a kitchen table. Keep portions small, let people choose whether to participate and retain the original packaging so ingredients and product information stay available.
